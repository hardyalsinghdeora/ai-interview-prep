# Study Plan — Engineering Manager

For tech leads and managers preparing for an Engineering Manager loop at a product company, SaaS vendor or growing startup; assumes about 10–12 hours a week for six weeks.

Read the role overview first: [README.md — The role](./README.md#the-role) and the [typical interview loop](./README.md#typical-interview-loop). EM loops are decided mainly by specific evidence: how you have hired, given feedback, handled underperformance and delivered through others. Most loops also check your technical judgement. This plan turns your experience into strong stories and working documents, while keeping system design fresh. If you have not yet managed people, use examples from mentoring, leading projects and interviewing, and say honestly what you have not yet done.

## Before you start

Spend 30 minutes rating yourself honestly. Skip or skim areas you rate 4 or 5.

| Skill area | Rate yourself 1–5 | What a 4 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Your management approach | | You can explain what you are accountable for and how you would tell you are doing well, with evidence. |
| 1:1s and feedback | | You can describe your 1:1 practice, and show how a piece of corrective feedback changed behaviour. |
| Performance management | | You can find the cause of underperformance, run a fair PIP, and tell a real story about a hard case. |
| Hiring | | You can design a loop and a scorecard, run a debrief, and explain how you reduce bias. |
| Retention and team health | | You can diagnose low morale, handle a competing offer and put a cost on attrition. |
| Planning | | You can build a capacity plan and agree the split of work with product. |
| Delivery under pressure | | You can respond to an impossible date with options, and recover after missed deadlines. |
| Org design and budget | | You can explain Team Topologies, plan a reorganisation and cut spending sensibly. |
| Technical judgement | | You can run a system design, evaluate a proposal and explain your role in an incident. |
| Stakeholders | | You can give examples of working through a disagreement with a product manager and of delivering bad news upward. |

## 6-week plan

Assumes ~10–12 hours a week. Write answers from your own experience, and practise out loud, because hard conversations sound different when spoken. Do one system design outline a week throughout.

### Week 1 — Your approach and your story

**Goals:** a clear view of the job, and an inventory of your evidence.

- [ ] Read [The role and your approach — model answers](./README.md#the-role-and-your-approach-1).
- [ ] Read the [People management section of the cheatsheet](../../cheatsheets/engineering-management.md#people-management) and [Tech lead vs EM vs staff engineer](../../cheatsheets/engineering-management.md#tech-lead-vs-em-vs-staff-engineer).
- [ ] Study [10 — Hiring-manager round topics](../../topics/10-behavioral/ANSWERS.md#hiring-manager-round-topics).
- [ ] Drill the fundamentals of [Engineering management](../../skills/engineering-management.md).

**Deliverables:**
1. An evidence sheet: team sizes, retention, hires, promotions, delivery and reliability numbers, with confidential details removed.
2. A [30-60-90 day plan](./README.md#you-join-as-em-of-an-existing-team-what-do-you-do-in-your-first-30-60-and-90-days), including who you would meet and what you would leave unchanged.

**Mock:** a 30-minute conversation with a director: "Why management? What is your approach? Tell me about your current team."

**End of week check:** you can answer "how do you know you are doing well as a manager?" without mentioning your own technical output.

### Week 2 — Feedback, growth and performance

**Goals:** show that you handle difficult people situations directly and fairly.

- [ ] Read [1:1s, feedback and growth — model answers](./README.md#11s-feedback-and-growth-1) and [Performance management — model answers](./README.md#performance-management-1).
- [ ] Read the Senior / Principal section of [Technical leadership & behavioral](../../skills/technical-leadership-and-behavioral.md), and answer the EM questions out loud.
- [ ] Study [10 — Conflict & collaboration](../../topics/10-behavioral/ANSWERS.md#conflict--collaboration).

**Deliverables:**
1. A performance review and an outline PIP for a fictional engineer, with dated evidence and measurable goals.
2. A script for a corrective feedback conversation, using situation, behaviour and impact.

**Mock:** a 30-minute role-play: a partner plays [the strong engineer who is dismissive in reviews](./README.md#your-strongest-engineer-is-dismissive-in-code-reviews-and-two-others-have-asked-to-move-teams-what-do-you-do) and pushes back. Then swap, and discuss how each felt.

**End of week check:** you can tell a real underperformance story, including what you would now do sooner.

### Week 3 — Hiring, onboarding and retention

**Goals:** show you can build and keep a team.

- [ ] Read [Hiring and onboarding — model answers](./README.md#hiring-and-onboarding-1) and [Retention and team health — model answers](./README.md#retention-and-team-health-1).
- [ ] Read the [Hiring section of the cheatsheet](../../cheatsheets/engineering-management.md#hiring).
- [ ] Drill the working depth of [Engineering management](../../skills/engineering-management.md), and the hiring questions in [Technical leadership & behavioral](../../skills/technical-leadership-and-behavioral.md).

**Deliverables:**
1. A hiring scorecard for a senior engineer, with signals for each rating and a clear hire or no-hire rule.
2. A one-page headcount request, with cost and the effect of refusal.
3. An attrition cost estimate, using your own or realistic numbers.

**Mock:** a 45-minute hiring round: design a loop, run a debrief in which interviewers disagree, then discuss [inheriting a team with low morale](./README.md#you-inherit-a-team-with-low-morale-and-two-resignations-in-the-last-quarter-what-do-you-do).

**End of week check:** you can explain how your loop tests each competency, and what you would do about a biased debrief.

### Week 4 — Planning and delivery

**Goals:** handle execution questions with numbers and options.

- [ ] Read [Planning and delivery — model answers](./README.md#planning-and-delivery-1).
- [ ] Read [Planning and prioritisation](../../cheatsheets/engineering-management.md#planning-and-prioritisation) and [Delivery and productivity metrics](../../cheatsheets/engineering-management.md#delivery-and-productivity-metrics).
- [ ] Drill [Tech lead & agile delivery](../../skills/tech-lead-and-agile-delivery.md), and read the [Tech Lead guide's planning answers](../19-tech-lead/README.md#planning-estimation-and-delivery-1).
- [ ] Study [10 — Prioritisation & pressure](../../topics/10-behavioral/ANSWERS.md#prioritisation--pressure).

**Deliverables:**
1. A [one-quarter capacity plan](./README.md#how-do-you-calculate-a-teams-real-capacity-for-a-quarter) in a spreadsheet, with a roadmap mapped onto it.
2. Team OKRs with baselines and targets.
3. A recovery note for "[three missed deadlines](./README.md#your-team-has-missed-three-deadlines-in-a-row-how-do-you-diagnose-it-and-recover)", written for leadership.

**Mock:** a 45-minute execution round: a partner plays a VP who has [promised a date](./README.md#leadership-has-promised-a-date-and-your-team-estimates-twice-the-time-available-what-do-you-do) that is impossible.

**End of week check:** you bring options with costs and a recommendation, rather than a yes or a no.

### Week 5 — Technical judgement, org design and budget

**Goals:** show enough technical depth, and think beyond a single team.

- [ ] Read [Technical judgement — model answers](./README.md#technical-judgement-1) and [Org design and budget — model answers](./README.md#org-design-and-budget-1).
- [ ] Work prompts from [03 — C. Classic system design](../../topics/03-system-design/ANSWERS.md#c-classic-system-design-still-asked), and the [follow-up questions](../../topics/03-system-design/ANSWERS.md#d-follow-up-questions-to-expect).
- [ ] Read [Org design](../../cheatsheets/engineering-management.md#org-design), [Cloud budget and FinOps](../../cheatsheets/engineering-management.md#cloud-budget-and-finops) and the [Observability & reliability cheatsheet](../../cheatsheets/observability-and-reliability.md).
- [ ] Drill [AI-assisted engineering](../../skills/ai-assisted-engineering.md), and the fundamentals of [System design](../../skills/system-design.md).

**Deliverables:**
1. A team design for [reorganising 40 engineers](./README.md#after-a-product-pivot-you-must-reorganise-40-engineers-how-do-you-design-and-roll-out-the-change), with a communication plan.
2. A ranked list of options for the [15% spending cut](./README.md#you-are-told-to-cut-engineering-spend-by-15-mid-year-what-do-you-do), with savings, risk and what would stop.

**Mock:** a 45-minute system design at EM level. Ask the partner to probe trade-offs, team structure, on-call and cost.

**End of week check:** you can draw and explain one system you led, and answer honestly when you reach the limit of what you know.

### Week 6 — Stakeholders, behavioral and full loop

**Goals:** polished stories, good partnership examples, and a full rehearsal.

- [ ] Read [Stakeholders — model answers](./README.md#stakeholders-1) and [Behavioral — model answers](./README.md#behavioral-1).
- [ ] Study [10 — Ownership & impact](../../topics/10-behavioral/ANSWERS.md#ownership--impact), [Failure & learning](../../topics/10-behavioral/ANSWERS.md#failure--learning) and [Values, ethics & culture](../../topics/10-behavioral/ANSWERS.md#values-ethics--culture).
- [ ] Prepare questions: [14 — To the hiring manager](../../topics/14-questions-to-ask/ANSWERS.md#to-the-hiring-manager) and [About growth](../../topics/14-questions-to-ask/ANSWERS.md#about-growth).
- [ ] Tick off the role's [Prep checklist](./README.md#prep-checklist).

**Deliverable:** a story matrix: rows are hiring, underperformance, managing out, conflict, retention, growing someone, a missed date, a difficult stakeholder, an incident, and your own mistake; each row has a real story with your actions and a measured result.

**Mock:** a full mock loop: people management (45), execution (45), system design (45), a cross-functional conversation with someone playing a product manager (30) and a behavioral round where the interviewer keeps asking "what exactly did you do?" (45).

**End of week check:** every story has the human side as well as the result, and survives three follow-up questions.

## Daily routine

A realistic 90-minute weekday:

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–30 | The week's topic: one README model answer, then write your own version from real experience. |
| 30–60 | One scenario out loud, recorded, using a question from a skill list. |
| 60–75 | Twice a week, a system design outline; otherwise, write part of the week's deliverable. |
| 75–90 | One story out loud; tighten your actions, the human side and the result. |

Weekends: finish the week's deliverable, and do the mock.

## Mock interview schedule

| Week | Mock | With whom |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Management approach conversation | A director or an experienced EM |
| 2 | Difficult conversation role-play | A peer manager who will push back |
| 3 | Hiring round with a debrief | An experienced hiring manager |
| 4 | Execution round with date pressure | A senior leader, or a peer playing a VP |
| 5 | EM system design | A staff engineer or a technical EM; Exponent offers EM mocks |
| 6 | Full loop | Two or three partners, including a product manager |

Do at least one role-play every week, and record one story a week.
